## What This Does

This classifier sorts posts from r/unpopularopinion into three categories: `analysis`, `hot_take`, and `reaction`. The community is built around contrarian opinions, which makes it a good source of confident claims with varying levels of evidence behind them. The classifier distinguishes posts that argue from facts from those that assert without support, and from those that are immediate emotional responses.

---

## Label Taxonomy

### `analysis`

**Definition:** Makes an argument backed by a specific, checkable fact — a stat, a date, a quote, or a named comparison — where the fact carries the argument.

**Example 1:**
> *Tesla's operating margin fell from 16.0% in 2022 to 8.2% in 2023, showing that aggressive EV price cuts directly eroded profitability.*

**Example 2:**
> *Spotify pays rightsholders $0.003 to $0.005 per stream, meaning an artist requires roughly 250,000 monthly plays to clear US minimum wage.*

### `hot_take`

**Definition:** A confident claim with no support offered. May sound authoritative, but the argument rests on assertion alone.

**Example 1:**
> *Samsung is better than Apple.*

**Example 2:**
> *The comic book movie boom irreversibly damaged Hollywood's capacity to fund mid-budget adult drama.*

### `reaction`

**Definition:** An immediate emotional response to something that just happened, focused on the writer's own experience rather than a general claim.

**Example 1:**
> *I just spilled an entire mug of piping hot coffee across my brand new mechanical keyboard and I am devastated.*

**Example 2:**
> *WAIT THEY FINALLY DROPPED THE TRAILER FOR GTA 6?! MY JAW IS ON THE FLOOR RIGHT NOW.*

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `analysis` vs `hot_take`

**The decision rule I used every time:** If the post names a specific checkable fact, ask whether the fact is doing the argumentative work or just decorating a conclusion the writer already reached. A stat that is load-bearing makes it `analysis`; a stat that is incidental makes it `hot_take`.

## The Dataset

**Where the posts came from:** r/unpopularopinion, supplemented with posts generated by Gemini to fill out underrepresented label counts.

**How I labelled them:** Gemini pre-labelled a batch of the generated posts and I read and corrected every one before including them. The Reddit posts were labelled cold.

**Counts per label:**

| Label | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| hot_take | 77 | 37.0% |
| analysis | 71 | 34.1% |
| reaction | 60 | 28.8% |
| **Total** | **208** | 100% |

**Three hard cases**

**1.**
> *The post:* "Buying expensive brand-name wine is completely foolish when blind taste tests prove cheap bottles win."
>
> *Could have been:* `analysis` — it references blind taste tests, which is a real and checkable finding.
>
> *I chose `hot_take`, because:* no specific study is cited. "Blind taste tests prove" is vague enough that the fact isn't load-bearing — it's shorthand for a general impression, not an argument from evidence.

**2.**
> *The post:* "Pineapples on pizza isn't a culinary crime; it's a masterclass in molecular gastrophysics because ethyl butyrate in the fruit chemically amplifies the ester profile of the cheese, even if traditionalists hate it."
>
> *Could have been:* `hot_take` — the framing is opinionated and the conclusion (pineapple belongs on pizza) is a strong personal stance.
>
> *I chose `analysis`, because:* the ethyl butyrate mechanism is a specific, checkable chemical fact and it is the actual reason given for the claim, not decoration.

**3.**
> *The post:* "The blinding flash of anger when somebody cuts across three highway lanes without a blinker is indescribable."
>
> *Could have been:* `hot_take` — it describes a universal experience in general terms rather than reporting something that just happened to the writer.
>
> *I chose `reaction`, because:* the post describes a felt emotional state in the moment and is focused on the writer's experience, not making a general claim about road safety or driver behaviour.

## The Training Run

**Base model:** `distilbert-base-uncased`

**Settings:** 3 epochs, learning rate 2e-5, batch size 16, seed 42, max 128 tokens per post

**Anything I changed from the defaults, and why:** Nothing. The defaults are designed for this dataset size and I had no prior reason to adjust them.

**Split sizes:** 70/15/15 stratified. Training: ~145 posts. Validation: ~31 posts. Test: 32 posts (analysis: 11, hot_take: 12, reaction: 9). Reaction is the smallest label in the test split at 9 — its F1 will be the most sensitive to seed choice next unit.

## How I Used AI

**Moment 1**

- *What I asked for:* Asked Gemini to generate additional posts in the style of r/unpopularopinion for each of the three labels, to supplement the Reddit posts I had collected.
- *What came back:* 50–60 posts per label, mostly well-formed but with some that were too on-the-nose or that mixed label characteristics.
- *What I changed:* Discarded any generated post where the label wasn't clear-cut and corrected labels on several where Gemini had assigned the wrong one, particularly on the `analysis`/`hot_take` boundary.

**Moment 2**

- *What I asked for:* Asked Gemini to help me write the label definitions in plain language, given the three label names and some examples.
- *What came back:* Wordy definitions that described the labels generally without capturing the decision rule.
- *What I changed:* Rewrote the definitions myself to be shorter and to explicitly name what separates each label from its nearest neighbour, especially the load-bearing fact rule for `analysis` vs `hot_take`.

**Pre-labelling disclosure:** Gemini pre-labelled the batch of generated posts. I read and corrected every label before including any post in the dataset. The Reddit posts were labelled cold without model assistance.

## Baseline vs. Trained

<!-- Both models on the same posts. `python baseline.py --trained results.json`
     prints this table for you. -->

| Measure | Baseline | Trained | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall accuracy | 0.594 | 0.969 | +0.375 |
| Macro F1 | 0.529 | 0.971 | +0.442 |
| F1 — `analysis` | 0.800 | 0.957 | +0.157 |
| F1 — `hot_take` | 0.606 | 0.957 | +0.350 |
| F1 — `reaction` | 0.182 | 1.000 | +0.818 |

**What I predicted before I looked:**
The trained model should beat the baseline on overall accuracy (≥0.70 trained vs likely 0.50–0.60 baseline) and especially on the `analysis`/`hot_take` boundary, since that distinction requires understanding argumentative structure — something fine-tuning should encode better than zero-shot prompting from bare definitions.

**What the gap actually means:**
Fine-tuning added a great deal: +0.375 accuracy and +0.442 macro F1. The most striking finding is `reaction`: the zero-shot model almost completely failed on it (F1 0.182) despite a clear definition, while the fine-tuned model got every reaction post right (F1 1.000). This suggests the written definition alone ("an immediate response to something that just happened") is not enough to reliably distinguish reaction posts — the model needed examples of what that actually looks like in practice. The prediction about `analysis`/`hot_take` was also correct: fine-tuning added +0.350 on `hot_take`, the label that depends on the load-bearing-fact rule that is hard to operationalise from words alone.

**Diagnoses**

No criteria were missed, so there are no misses to diagnose. What the results reveal instead:

**Why the model performed so well:** The three labels are behaviourally distinct enough that even 200 examples and 3 epochs produced near-ceiling accuracy. `reaction` was classified perfectly across all three seeds (F1 1.000), which was the most surprising result — the zero-shot baseline got `reaction` almost entirely wrong (F1 0.182), showing the model genuinely learned something the definition alone couldn't convey. `analysis` at seed 2024 (F1 0.900) was the weakest individual number, which matches expectation: the `analysis`/`hot_take` boundary is the hardest in the taxonomy and seed 2024 drew a slightly harder split.

**What the agreement report says about labelling consistency:** 100% agreement with staff on 30 posts, including the four I identified as genuinely hard (sp_007, sp_010, sp_014, sp_027), suggests my load-bearing-fact rule was applied consistently on posts where I had a written decision rule. The agreement report cannot confirm this for the 200 posts in my own dataset, because there is no second annotator for those — but the absence of disagreements on boundary cases in the staff set is the closest evidence available that my labelling was not systematically inconsistent on the `analysis`/`hot_take` pair.

## Agreement Report

     Remember you labelled these 30 under the STAFF taxonomy in

     from those definitions and those decision rules. -->

**Agreement rate:** 30 / 30 = 100%

I matched staff on every post. There were no disagreements to adjudicate.

**Was the set easy, or is the rule genuinely tight?**

Both, partially. The staff taxonomy is unusually well-constructed: rule 1 (does the fact carry the argument?) gives a clean test for the hardest boundary (`analysis` vs `hot_take`), and rule 2 (is it about a claim or about the writer?) handles `reaction` vs `hot_take` without much grey area. The posts I found hardest, and why my rule put them where it did:

- **sp_007** ("Ran the same loop in 4C and in 24C... 90 seconds slower"): Could feel like a `reaction` because it just happened and the writer is describing their own run. Rule 3 settles it — recency doesn't outrank evidence, and the 90-second number over a controlled same-effort loop is load-bearing. → `analysis`.

- **sp_010** ("Three of us ran the same session, different shoes, within 10 seconds"): Anecdotal, small sample. But the data is explicitly doing the argumentative work — the writer's conclusion ("whatever shoes do was smaller than the noise between three people") is derived from the comparison. → `analysis`.

- **sp_014** ("Times are down 3%... so obviously the shoes are the whole story"): The one I was most ready to argue. The taxonomy's own example is nearly identical ("Super shoes have ruined racing, times are down 3% since 2017" → `hot_take`). The word "obviously" signals the conclusion was pre-reached; the 3% decorates it. → `hot_take`.

- **sp_027** ("Wind was so bad I was leaning at 20 degrees"): "20 degrees" looks specific but it is describing a felt experience mid-race, not constructing an argument. The rest of the post ("coming back felt like cheating") is about the writer's own sensation. → `reaction`.

**What the pattern tells me:**

No disagreements means either the rules are tight enough to produce consistent application, or the set happened to avoid the genuinely hard cases. Probably both. The taxonomy's decision rules are more explicit than the ones I wrote for my own labels — particularly the load-bearing-fact rule and the recency-doesn't-outrank-evidence rule. Applied consistently, they left almost no room for ambiguity on any of the 30 posts. That is itself a diagnostic: the cases where my own labels are likely to be inconsistent are the ones where I did not write that kind of explicit tie-break.

## The Improvement

**What I changed:** Increased training epochs from 3 to 5. Everything else (learning rate, batch size, data, labels) unchanged.

**Which diagnosis pointed at it:** The before run showed `analysis` F1 dropping to 0.900 at seed 2024, the lowest single value across all labels and seeds. The diagnosis identified the `analysis`/`hot_take` boundary as the hardest to learn and noted seed 2024 drew a harder split. More training passes was the most direct way to give the model more time on that boundary without changing the data.

**Did it help, and how do I know:**

Mixed result. The specific weakness the improvement targeted — `analysis` F1 at seed 2024 — improved from 0.900 to 0.952, and `analysis` F1 is now identical across all three seeds (0.952/0.952/0.952, spread 0.000). That part worked.

But seed 7 regressed: `hot_take` fell from 0.960 to 0.917 and `reaction` fell from 1.000 to 0.947. The accuracy for seed 7 dropped from 0.969 to 0.938. The extra epochs over-fitted the seed 7 split on some boundary the 3-epoch run had got right. The before run had a lower floor but a higher ceiling at seed 7; the after run is more consistent on `analysis` but less consistent overall.

All five criteria are still met in both runs. The improvement is real but narrow — it fixed one label's floor at the cost of introducing a regression elsewhere. The honest conclusion is that 3 epochs was close to optimal for this dataset size and the 5-epoch run traded one weakness for another.



---

## What's Still Broken

No criterion was missed in either the before or after run, so there is nothing left broken in terms of the five targets.

The one remaining weakness is seed-to-seed variance on the `analysis`/`hot_take` boundary. The before run had a floor of 0.900 for `analysis`; the after run raised that floor but introduced a `hot_take` floor of 0.917 at seed 7. Neither is below the 0.55 target, but both show the boundary is still the hardest for the model. To fix it properly — rather than just trading one floor for another — I would add more training examples that sit squarely on the boundary: posts that cite a statistic but where the statistic is decorative rather than load-bearing. The model would benefit from more signal on exactly that case. I stopped because adding data was not permitted in this unit.

**The gap between what I meant my labels to capture and what the model learned:**

My label definitions distinguished `analysis` from `hot_take` by whether a fact carries the argument or merely decorates it — a distinction that requires reading the structure of a claim, not just detecting the presence of a number. The confusion matrix (seed 42) shows the model got this nearly right: only 1 post crossed the boundary in either direction. But the `analysis` F1 variability across seeds (0.957→0.952→0.900 before; 0.952 flat after) tells me the model is learning a coarser signal — probably something like "does this post contain a number" — rather than the load-bearing-fact rule, because a coarser rule would fluctuate more with which specific posts land in the test split.
